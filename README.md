## Description

Incident handling and threat containment are among the most critical operational disciplines in modern cybersecurity. As cyber attacks increase in frequency and complexity, organizations can no longer rely solely on preventive controls. They require skilled Incident Handlers who understand the exact tools and techniques used by attackers, enabling them to detect breaches early, contain active threats, and thoroughly remediate compromised environments.

Global enterprise organizations, financial institutions, government entities, defense contractors, and top-tier SOCs recognize the GIAC Certified Incident Handler (GCIH) certification as a gold standard credential. Earning this certification proves your ability to manage security incidents using structured methodologies, analyze real-world hacker tactics, and defend networks against advanced attacker TTPs (Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures).

My course explains the core methodologies, investigation techniques, and technical skills aligned with the SANS SEC504 / GCIH curriculum to help you understand how attackers breach networks and how incident response teams discover, contain, and eradicate those threats.

My course is structured into 2 parts:

* the first one is about Incident Response Methodology, Reconnaissance, and Initial Access Vectors. The 6-step Incident Handling process (Preparation, Identification, Containment, Eradication, Recovery, Lessons Learned), legal issues in computer crime, drive-by attacks, password attacks, public-facing service exploitation, weaponization, network scanning and enumeration, Wi-Fi attack vectors, and web application attack identification are discussed.
* the second part of the course is all about Endpoint Exploitation, Post-Exploitation, Persistence, and Incident Remediation. The topics cover aspects like: Command-line attack execution, PowerShell and Living off the Land (LotL) tactics, privilege escalation techniques on Windows and Linux, credential harvesting (LSASS dumping, Mimikatz, password hashes), lateral movement mechanisms (PsExec, WMI, WinRM), persistence mechanisms (Scheduled Tasks, Registry keys, services), hiding data and covering tracks, log analysis, memory forensics basics, network traffic analysis for C2 communications, and executing effective containment, eradication, and post-incident reporting.

After going through all the lessons of this course you will have a solid knowledge of practical incident response, how to analyze attacker tools and artifacts, and how to successfully prepare for the GIAC Certified Incident Handler (GCIH) certification exam.

With the information here you can:

* work as an Incident Responder, Incident Handler, or SOC Tier 2/3 Specialist;
* lead Computer Incident Response Teams (CIRT) and Digital Forensics and Incident Response (DFIR) engagements;
* conduct compromise assessments, threat containment, and post-breach eradication for enterprise environments; or
* if you are a SOC Manager, IT Director, or System Administrator you will know how to establish incident response plans, minimize breach impact, and harden systems against real-world attack vectors.

If none of the options above suits your profile you can use the information in my course to gain deep technical insights into hacker tools and incident handling, significantly advancing your offensive and defensive cybersecurity capabilities.
